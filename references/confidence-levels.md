# Confidence Level Definitions

The Bible Exegesis Agent tags every interpretive claim with one of four
confidence levels. This document defines each level and provides guidance
on when to apply them.

## Levels

### High confidence
The interpretation has strong support from grammar, syntax, and context.
There is broad agreement among interpreters, and alternative readings are
significantly weaker.

**Criteria (meet most):**
- Grammar unambiguously supports this reading
- Context (immediate and broader) aligns
- No serious competing interpretations
- Original-language evidence is clear

**Example:** "The subject of Philippians 2:6 is Christ" — grammatically
unambiguous from the preceding verse.

### Plausible
A reasonable interpretation with good but not decisive support. Some ambiguity
exists, but this reading has more evidence in its favor than alternatives.

**Criteria:**
- Grammar supports this reading but doesn't exclude others
- Context leans toward this interpretation
- One or two competing readings exist but are weaker

**Example:** "The 'thorn in the flesh' (2 Cor 12:7) is likely a physical
ailment" — contextual clues support it, but the text doesn't specify.

### Ambiguous
Multiple viable readings exist, and the available evidence does not clearly
favor one over the others. The text may be intentionally polyvalent.

**Criteria:**
- Grammar permits multiple readings
- Context does not decisively narrow the options
- The ambiguity may be deliberate

**Example:** "Whether 'faith of Christ' (pistis Christou) is subjective
genitive ('Christ's faithfulness') or objective genitive ('faith in Christ')
cannot be determined from grammar alone."

### Disputed
Known, significant scholarly disagreement exists. Different interpretive
traditions read this text differently, and the disagreement often reflects
deeper methodological or theological commitments.

**Criteria:**
- Major interpretive traditions diverge
- The disagreement is well-documented
- Resolution depends on assumptions beyond the text itself

**Example:** "The identity of 'the restrainer' in 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7 is
disputed — proposed identifications include the Roman Empire, the Holy Spirit,
the preaching of the gospel, and others."

## Usage rules

1. **Always state the confidence level** when making an interpretive claim
2. **Do not inflate confidence** — if there is genuine ambiguity, say so
3. **Do not deflate confidence** — if the grammar is clear, say that too
4. **Separate observation from interpretation** — observations (what the
   grammar shows) do not need confidence tags; interpretations (what it means)
   always do
5. **Describe the disagreement** when the level is "disputed" — briefly present
   the major positions without naming scholars (unless asked)
